# 1. Game Overview

## 1.1 Concept

WILDCALL is a cozy, stylized open-world naturalist RPG in which the player starts as an amateur birdwatcher and gradually becomes an accomplished naturalist.

The player explores forests, wetlands, mountains, coastlines, and other natural environments. Through curiosity, patience, observation, and exploration, they discover wildlife, identify bird species, document encounters, complete research assignments, improve their skills, acquire equipment, and unlock new areas of the world.

Birds are not simply collectibles placed throughout the world. They are living creatures with their own routines, habitats, behaviors, territories, and reactions to the player.

The player must actively discover wildlife by:

- Listening to environmental sounds.
- Recognizing unfamiliar bird calls.
- Investigating the source of sounds.
- Searching trees, shrubs, reeds, and other habitats.
- Observing movement and silhouettes.
- Using binoculars and other equipment.
- Waiting patiently for animals to reveal themselves.
- Identifying species based on evidence.
- Photographing, recording, and documenting discoveries.

Progression is built around knowledge, field experience, equipment, exploration, and personal discoveries rather than combat.

## 1.2 Core Identity

**WILDCALL is a cozy wildlife exploration game with the structure and progression of an RPG, where curiosity is the primary gameplay mechanic.**

The game combines:

- Open-world exploration.
- Birdwatching and wildlife discovery.
- RPG progression.
- Equipment collection and upgrades.
- Photography and audio recording.
- Research and identification.
- Quests and environmental storytelling.
- A living ecosystem.
- Personal collection and journal progression.

## 1.3 Player Fantasy

The player should feel like a real naturalist exploring an unfamiliar wilderness.

The emotional experience should be built around moments such as:

- Hearing a strange call in the distance.
- Wondering which bird is making the sound.
- Following it through the forest.
- Finding movement in the canopy.
- Carefully raising binoculars.
- Recognizing the species.
- Capturing a beautiful photograph.
- Discovering unusual behavior.
- Finding a hidden location.
- Returning to an area and discovering something new.
- Gradually becoming more knowledgeable and capable.

The player should feel that their discoveries are earned through their own curiosity and attention.

# 4. Core Gameplay Loop

## 4.1 Primary Gameplay Loop

**Explore → Listen → Investigate → Spot → Document → Progress**

### Explore

The player walks through the world, discovers locations, learns the environment, and encounters wildlife.

### Listen

The player detects bird calls and environmental sounds.

Sound may provide clues about:

- Direction.
- Distance.
- Pitch.
- Repetition.
- Species characteristics.
- Habitat.

### Investigate

The player determines where the sound originates.

They may:

- Walk toward it.
- Listen again.
- Use audio equipment.
- Inspect the environment.
- Search nearby trees.
- Follow movement.
- Change their approach.

### Spot

The player physically locates the bird.

This may require:

- Recognizing movement.
- Finding a silhouette.
- Identifying a color pattern.
- Using binoculars.
- Adjusting position.
- Waiting for movement.

### Document

The player records their discovery through:

- Photography.
- Audio recordings.
- Written observations.
- Species identification.

### Progress

The player earns:

- XP.
- Journal progress.
- Skill progress.
- Quest progress.
- Reputation.
- Equipment unlocks.
- Currency.
- New opportunities.

# 5. Audio-Led Bird Discovery

Audio is one of the most important and unique gameplay systems in WILDCALL.

## 5.1 Discovery Philosophy

Birds should often be heard before they are seen.

An unfamiliar sound creates curiosity and motivates investigation.

The player should think:

> "What is making that sound?"

Rather than:

> "There is a bird marker over there."

## 5.2 Bird Detection

When the player hears a potentially relevant sound, the HUD may provide subtle information.

Possible clues:

| Clue | Example |
|---|---|
| Direction | Northeast |
| Distance | Approximately 30–45 m |
| Pitch | High |
| Pattern | Repeated |
| Habitat | Woodland canopy |
| Confidence | Uncertain |

The interface should not automatically reveal the species.

## 5.3 Spatial Audio

Bird calls should use spatial audio to communicate:

- Direction.
- Distance.
- Elevation.
- Movement.
- Environmental obstruction.

The player should be able to hear that a bird is:

- Somewhere to their left.
- Far away.
- Above them.
- Behind dense foliage.
- Moving between locations.

Environmental sounds should sometimes obscure bird calls.

## 5.4 Focused Listening

The player may enter a focused listening mode.

Possible effects:

- Player movement slows.
- Environmental UI becomes less prominent.
- Audio layers become easier to distinguish.
- Directional information becomes clearer.
- The player can listen for quieter calls.

Focused listening must not magically highlight birds.

It improves the player's ability to interpret audio rather than revealing wildlife automatically.

## 5.5 Audio Equipment

Equipment may improve:

- Recording range.
- Directional accuracy.
- Audio clarity.
- Noise reduction.
- Frequency analysis.
- Identification assistance.

Potential equipment:

- Basic audio recorder.
- Directional microphone.
- Parabolic microphone.
- Headphones.
- Audio analyzer.
- Spectrogram device.
- Advanced field recorder.

## 5.6 Rare Audio Encounters

Rare encounters should not be announced.

A rare bird may produce a distinctive sound that encourages the player to investigate.

The player should discover rarity naturally.

# 6. Bird Spotting Mechanics

Bird spotting should be a skill rather than a simple interaction.

## 6.1 Visual Discovery

Birds may be difficult to see because of:

- Dense foliage.
- Branches.
- Distance.
- Movement.
- Lighting.
- Weather.
- Small size.
- Camouflage.

Players discover birds through:

- Movement.
- Silhouettes.
- Color flashes.
- Shape.
- Behavior.
- Visual patterns.

## 6.2 Binocular Mechanics

Binoculars introduce tradeoffs.

Possible characteristics:

- Magnification.
- Field of view.
- Stability.
- Focus speed.
- Clarity.
- Weight.

Higher magnification provides more detail but can result in:

- Narrower field of view.
- More hand sway.
- Difficulty locating targets.
- Difficulty tracking moving birds.

Lower magnification may make it easier to find small birds in dense foliage.

## 6.3 Patience

Birds may:

- Move between branches.
- Hide.
- Stop calling.
- Fly away.
- Return later.

Players can wait for:

- Movement.
- Better visibility.
- A clearer angle.
- A bird to emerge from foliage.
- A bird to return to a perch.

## 6.4 Approach Behavior

The player can approach wildlife using different movement styles.

| Player Behavior | Wildlife Reaction |
|---|---|
| Sprint | Highly alert; likely to flee |
| Run | Alert |
| Walk | Cautious |
| Crouch | Less noticeable |
| Stand still | Wildlife gradually settles |
| Direct approach | Increased fear |
| Indirect approach | Reduced fear |
| Remain nearby | Some species may habituate |

This is not intended to become a stealth game.

The purpose is to reward patience and naturalist behavior.

# 35. Core Design Rules

These principles should guide future design decisions.

## Rule 1 — Discovery Must Be Earned

Wildlife should not be revealed automatically.

The player should actively investigate.

## Rule 2 — Audio Is Gameplay

Bird calls are information, clues, and motivation.

Audio should not simply be background decoration.

## Rule 3 — Knowledge Is Progression

The player should become better at identifying and understanding wildlife.

Progression should feel like the player becoming a naturalist.

## Rule 4 — Equipment Changes Playstyle

Equipment should provide meaningful advantages and disadvantages.

## Rule 5 — The World Exists Without the Player

Wildlife should have routines, habitats, and behaviors independent of player actions.

## Rule 6 — Exploration Is Always Valuable

Off-trail exploration should provide meaningful discoveries.

## Rule 7 — Rare Discoveries Should Feel Special

Rare encounters should be based on environmental conditions, behavior, and player discovery rather than constant notifications.

## Rule 8 — The Game Must Remain Cozy

Difficulty should come from observation, patience, knowledge, and exploration—not punishment.

## Rule 9 — Support Different Naturalist Playstyles

Players should be able to focus on:

- Photography.
- Audio.
- Identification.
- Exploration.
- Research.
- Collection.

No single playstyle should be mandatory.

## Rule 10 — Curiosity Is the Main Reward

The player should always have a reason to wonder what is around the next corner, beyond the next trail, or hidden in the trees.

# 36. Final Vision

WILDCALL is a game about slowing down and paying attention to the natural world.

The player begins with basic equipment, limited knowledge, and a small area to explore. Through listening, observing, investigating, and documenting wildlife, they gradually develop into a skilled naturalist.

The world is not filled with markers telling the player where to go.

Instead, it is filled with clues.

A sound in the distance.

Movement between branches.

A trail leading somewhere unfamiliar.

A species behaving differently.

A hidden observation point.

A rare bird visiting the wrong habitat.

A forgotten research station.

A location that sounds different at dawn.

Every discovery contributes to the player's personal journey.

Progression gives the player better equipment, deeper knowledge, new skills, and access to more challenging environments—but the most important progression is the player's own familiarity with the world.

**The ultimate goal is not to complete a checklist of birds. It is to become someone who understands the world well enough to discover things others would never notice.**

---

## Project North Star

> **WILDCALL should make players curious about the world around them—even when they are not playing the game.**

The game should encourage players to listen more closely to birds outside, notice movement in trees, recognize species in their own environment, and develop a genuine interest in wildlife and nature.

That is the experience the game should ultimately deliver.
